# Mission parameters and verification — research findings (2026)

Scope: DJI Mini 5 Pro + DJI RC2 + DJI Fly, `drone-flightplan` (HOT OSM) generating WPML KMZ, ODM + splatfacto deliverables, construction sites in Canada. This file distinguishes **Confirmed** (stated in a primary source, cited inline) from **Inferred** (my synthesis, or a secondary/blog source, flagged as such). Anything not established is listed in "Could not verify."

---

## Recommendation

1. **Overlap.** Keep 80/70 as the floor for uniform/vegetated construction sites, but go to 85/85 whenever the site is mostly bare earth, gravel, fresh concrete, or snow — Pix4D and ODM both single out low-texture surfaces as the case that needs the highest overlap, and doming/misalignment failures concentrate there. [Pix4D](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002471546), [ODM](https://docs.opendronemap.org/flying/).
2. **Keep the oblique passes, but change what they're for.** Current standard already does oblique passes alongside nadir at −80° gimbal — that's within Pix4D's recommended 45–80° double-grid range (70° typical) and ODM's 45° cross-grid guidance. This is good for doming control. It is **not** enough for splats: Pix4D's own Gaussian Splatting guidance for PIX4Dmatic calls for **circular orbits at varying altitudes and pitch angles**, not just an oblique grid pass, to get the view diversity splats need. **Add a dedicated orbit sub-mission per structure/site feature you want in the splat** (see below) rather than trying to make one grid serve both deliverables.
3. **For splat-quality captures**, plan orbits at 2–3 altitude rings, gimbal pitch stepped between roughly −30° and −60° per ring (avoid pure nadir and avoid pointing at open sky), one photo every 5–10° of arc (or continuous video at slow, steady angular rate), keeping the target framed and out of frame edges. This is a synthesis of Pix4D's PIX4Dmatic splatting guidance (orbit + helix patterns, varying altitude and pitch, avoid pure nadir, minimize sky) and the general aerial-3DGS/NeRF literature (view diversity, multi-elevation rings) — **inferred parameters**, not a single source's exact numbers; treat as a starting point to tune empirically with your own splatfacto runs.
4. **Extend the grid one pass beyond the boundary — keep doing this.** It is consistent with every source's overlap-degradation-at-edges concern, though no source gives it as a named rule; it is standard practice, not written down as a number anywhere I found.
5. **Q2's most decision-relevant finding: DJI Fly's consumer WPML is not the documented spec.** The WPML spec published at `github.com/dji-sdk/Cloud-API-Doc` explicitly scopes every element to enterprise aircraft (M300/M350/M30/M3E-T-M/M3D-TD) flown from DJI Pilot 2 / FlightHub 2 — the Mini 5 Pro is not listed anywhere in it, and reverse-engineered evidence (below) shows DJI Fly's consumer format differs from that spec in namespace and in at least one per-waypoint field. Do not treat the Cloud-API-Doc spec as ground truth for what DJI Fly on the Mini 5 Pro will actually parse — validate empirically against your own RC2's exported KMZ.
6. **Check your own tool's defaults now, not after a flight.** `hotosm/drone-flightplan`'s WPML writer (the tool named in your brief) hardcodes `exitOnRCLost = goContinue` (continue the mission on signal loss, not RTH) and — as of the version inspected — does **not** write a `template.kml` at all, only `waylines.wpml`, zipped alone into the KMZ. A different reverse-engineering project (FlyPath) found that DJI Fly rebuilds a mission's waylines from `template.kml` on save/cloud-sync and scrambles the route if that file is missing or lacks the waypoints. If your KMZs are loaded once and flown without ever being re-saved on the RC2, this may not bite you — but it is worth confirming on your own controller before relying on it, and worth deciding deliberately whether "continue on signal loss" is the behavior you want over a construction site. See Q2 for full detail and sourcing.
7. **DEM/obstacle data for Canada:** use NRCan's HRDEM (1 m/2 m, lidar-derived, STAC/WCS/WMS, Open Government Licence) where it has coverage, falling back to MRDEM (30 m, nationwide) and Copernicus GLO-30 (30 m, global, free) elsewhere. Building heights: NRCan's lidar-derived building layer (partial provincial coverage) or OSM `building:height`/`building:levels` (sparse, crowd-sourced) — no complete authoritative national source exists. Airspace: NAV CANADA's own data cannot be redistributed/re-used programmatically (confirmed), so use Transport Canada's open-government aerodrome/heliport shapefiles plus OpenAIP's crowd-sourced Canada airspace export as a practical (not authoritative) substitute; treat OpenAIP as directional only for anything safety-critical.
8. **Pre-flight verification:** no single confirmed open-source tool computes footprint/overlap/GSD-from-DEM specifically for DJI WPML missions; QGIS's "Flight Planner" plugin and PX4/ArduPilot-oriented QGroundControl both do this generically and could be adapted or used as a reference implementation to build your own overlap/GSD checker against the KMZ your pipeline emits.
9. **Post-flight verification:** pull `GPSLatitude/Longitude`, `AbsoluteAltitude`, `RelativeAltitude`, `GimbalPitchDegree`, `FlightYawDegree` from each photo's XMP (`drone-dji` namespace) with ExifTool (confirmed support for Mini 5 Pro tags) and diff against the planned waypoint/action list; use ODM's own quality report (overlap heatmap, GPS/GCP/3D reprojection error tables) as the processing-side check. Reprojection error < ~1 px mean is the commonly cited "healthy" threshold, but this comes from industry blog commentary, not a standards body — flagged as inferred/secondary below.

## Q1 — Recommended capture parameters by purpose

### Overlap by terrain type

- **Pix4D (general):** ≥75% frontal / ≥60% side overlap as the baseline; agriculture (flat fields) ≥80/80; forest/dense vegetation ≥85/85 (fly higher to help matching); thermal ≥90/90. **Confirmed.** [Image acquisition — PIX4Dmapper](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002471546)
- **OpenDroneMap:** 70–80% overlap slightly off-nadir (5–10°) for 2D/2.5D; 80–83% for complex buildings/vegetation; 60% nadir + 70–80% at 45° gimbal cross-grid for full 3D. Overlap should increase with altitude (e.g., 65/75 at 40 m vs 70/80 at 80 m). **Confirmed.** [Flying Tips — OpenDroneMap docs](https://docs.opendronemap.org/flying/)
- **Agisoft Metashape manual:** ≥60% side + ≥80% forward overlap for aerial surveys; more photos beats too few; minimize blind zones. **Confirmed.** [Metashape Aerial Survey Tips](https://www.agisoftmetashape.com/agisoft-metashape-aerial-survey-tips-how-to-capture-and-process-high-quality-data/), [Metashape 2.1 manual PDF](https://www.agisoft.com/pdf/metashape-pro_2_1_en.pdf)
- **USGS-adjacent practice:** USGS UAS data releases document ~70% forelap (along-track) and ~50% sidelap (cross-track) as flown, and separately ~70% between adjacent lines in other releases — these are as-flown descriptions in specific data-release metadata, not a published USGS National UAS Project Office standard I could locate. **Inferred/partial** — I could not find a single USGS NUSO or JAG memo setting a general overlap standard; see "Could not verify."
- **ASPRS:** the current **Positional Accuracy Standards for Digital Geospatial Data, Edition 2** (2023/2024) added a dedicated UAS addendum and switched exclusively to RMSE-based accuracy metrics (dropped 95%-confidence-level accuracy statements), but the actual overlap/flight-parameter recommendations are inside the paywalled standard document itself. **Confirmed that the standard and its UAS addendum exist**; **could not verify** its specific overlap numbers — see "Could not verify." [ASPRS standard listing](https://my.asprs.org/ASPRSMember/ASPRSMember/Store/StoreLayouts/Item_Detail.aspx?iProductCode=PAS2024&Category=PUB), [LIDAR Magazine overview](https://lidarmag.com/2025/06/30/overview-of-the-asprs-positional-accuracy-standards-for-digital-geospatial-data/)
- **Snow:** none of the primary sources above give snow-specific overlap numbers. Snow behaves like the "featureless/low-contrast surface" case (same failure mode as fresh concrete or bare gravel) that Pix4D and ODM both warn needs more overlap — **inferred by analogy**, not a direct citation. Practical guidance from the field (secondary/blog, aerocartwright.com) suggests 80–85%+ frontal for feature-poor surfaces generally; treat as a reasonable prior, not a confirmed standard.

### Altitude vs GSD

- ODM: higher altitude → larger footprint → larger (worse) GSD; for small-GSD requirements, fly at 3–4× the height of the tallest object in the scene. **Confirmed.** [High Precision Workflows — ODM docs](https://docs.opendronemap.org/map-accuracy/)
- ODM relative accuracy is typically 1–3× the average GSD; with GCPs, ~2.5× GSD horizontal / ~4× GSD vertical. **Confirmed**, same source.

### Grid pattern / cross-hatch / double grid

- Pix4D: regular single grid for most 2D mapping; **double grid** (two perpendicular passes) recommended for 3D outputs, surfaces with height variation, and buildings/forests, at the cost of ~2× flight time. Tilt angle 45–80° for double grid, ~70° typical. **Confirmed.** [Double Grid Mission Settings](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002496206), [Selecting the Image Acquisition Plan Type](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/202557459)
- ODM: recommends nadir passes *plus* a 45°-gimbal cross-grid pass for full 3D — nadir provides ortho texture, the angled pass ties the 3D model together. **Confirmed.** [Flying Tips — ODM](https://docs.opendronemap.org/flying/)

### Oblique angle and doming

- Pix4D: for building-facade capture, oblique images between 10° and 35° off nadir in a double-grid pattern. For a full building-reconstruction flight plan: first orbit at 45° camera angle, subsequent orbits at increasing altitude / decreasing camera angle, one photo every 5–10° of arc, and altitude should not vary more than 2× across orbits to keep GSD consistent. **Confirmed.** [Image acquisition — PIX4Dmapper](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002471546)
- Doming cause (Pix4D docs): more likely when tie-point quality is poor (featureless terrain, motion blur, inadequate overlap), when front overlap is below 75% or side overlap below 60%, or when all photos are nearly parallel (i.e., nadir-only). Oblique imagery gives the viewing-geometry diversity that makes radial-distortion/curvature mathematically separable from real terrain relief. **Confirmed** cause-and-effect statement is from Pix4D's docs; the explicit inference that "double grid + adequate overlap reduces doming" is **my synthesis** of that statement, not a separately-stated Pix4D claim. [Image acquisition — PIX4Dmapper](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002471546)

### Orbit / structure-scan parameters for 3D

- Pix4D building-reconstruction flight plan (above): radius isn't given as a number (it's derived from keeping the object in frame and holding overlap), angular step 5–10° per photo, 2 or more altitude rings with paired pitch angles, ≤2× altitude spread. **Confirmed**, same Pix4D source.
- Pix4D's **PIX4Dmatic Gaussian Splatting guidance** (the closest thing to an official vendor statement on drone capture for splats): 70–80% longitudinal / 60–70% transverse overlap; double-grid for nadir projects while varying pitch to see the scene from multiple directions; **circular orbits at varying altitudes with different gimbal pitch angles** to maximize viewpoint diversity; a **helix pattern** around vertical structures (e.g., towers); avoid pure nadir; minimize sky in frame (strong sky/ground contrast introduces artifacts). **Confirmed.** [Gaussian splats & point cloud — PIX4Dmatic](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/gaussian-splatting-pix4dmatic)
- Academic multi-elevation orbit example (not drone-specific, object-capture study): 5 camera elevations from near-ground to high aerial, 61 images evenly sampled per elevation circle. Cited as **one example from the literature** of what a multi-ring orbit looks like numerically, not a drone-photogrammetry standard. **Confirmed as a data point, not a general rule.**

### Gaussian Splatting / NeRF-specific drone guidance

- Nerfstudio-adjacent guidance (secondary, aggregated from vendor/community docs, not nerfstudio's own site directly): 100–300 photos, >60% overlap between adjacent frames; for drone work, ≥80% frontal / ≥70% side as a floor; fly at least one orbital or cross-hatch pass at 30–45° off nadir; reduce motion blur; view target from as many angles as possible. **Secondary source, treat as directional**, not a confirmed nerfstudio-authored standard (I could not load nerfstudio's own docs.nerf.studio page directly to confirm wording).
- DroneDeploy publishes a dedicated "Gaussian Splats" help article; I could not fetch its content (403 on WebFetch) — **could not verify** its specifics, listed below.
- Peer-reviewed aerial-3DGS work confirms: pure-nadir capture is a known failure mode for 3D surface quality; datasets built for aerial 3DGS/surface reconstruction typically use a nadir + several oblique viewpoints rig, sometimes at multiple flight heights when structures are tall. **Confirmed** general finding, from arXiv papers (not peer-reviewed-journal-certified, but citable primary research sources): [3D Gaussian Splatting for Large-scale Surface Reconstruction from Aerial Images](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.00381), [DRAGON: Drone and Ground Gaussian Splatting for 3D Building Reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01761), [ULSR-GS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01402).

**Bottom line for Q1:** your grid-plus-oblique standard is well supported for orthomosaic/ODM work. For splats, every vendor/paper source that specifically addresses splatting (Pix4D's own splatting doc, the aerial-3DGS papers) converges on the same missing ingredient versus a mapping grid: **orbit motion at more than one altitude/pitch combination**, not just one oblique pass layered on a nadir grid. That is the one architectural change to make, not a specific overlap number change.

## Q2 — DJI Mini 5 Pro / DJI Fly waypoint mission constraints (deep dive)

### 2.0 The central caveat: two different WPML dialects

The WPML spec DJI publishes at `github.com/dji-sdk/Cloud-API-Doc` is written for the **Cloud API / DJI Pilot 2 / FlightHub 2** stack. Its own "Product Support" column, present on every single documented element, lists only: **M300 RTK, M350 RTK, M30/M30T, M3E/M3T/M3M, M3D/M3TD**. The Mini 5 Pro (and every other sub-250g/consumer DJI Fly aircraft) is never listed. **Confirmed by direct inspection of the spec's tables** — [common-element.md](https://github.com/dji-sdk/Cloud-API-Doc/blob/master/docs/en/60.api-reference/00.dji-wpml/40.common-element.md), [template-kml.md](https://github.com/dji-sdk/Cloud-API-Doc/blob/master/docs/en/60.api-reference/00.dji-wpml/20.template-kml.md), [waylines-wpml.md](https://github.com/dji-sdk/Cloud-API-Doc/blob/master/docs/en/60.api-reference/00.dji-wpml/30.waylines-wpml.md).

DJI Fly's own consumer waypoint format is **not published anywhere by DJI**. The best evidence is reverse-engineering by two independent open-source projects, and they disagree on a basic structural fact (the XML namespace URI), which itself is an important finding:

| | Enterprise WPML (Cloud-API-Doc, DJI Pilot 2) | FlyPath's consumer writer (`wpml/consumer.py`) | `hotosm/drone-flightplan`'s writer (`wpml.py`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Namespace | `http://www.dji.com/wpmz/1.0.6` | `http://www.uav.com/wpmz/1.0.2` (non-DJI domain) | `http://www.dji.com/wpmz/1.0.2` |
| Basis | Official published spec | "Verified against a DJI Mini 4 Pro + DJI RC2 native mission dump" (source code comment) | Author's own reverse-engineering; commented-out template.kml sample in the same file also uses the `dji.com` namespace |
| `template.kml` written? | Yes, required | Yes — required, because DJI Fly rebuilds waylines from it on save/cloud-sync (see 2.6) | **No** — only `waylines.wpml` is written into the KMZ; the `template.kml`-generation code is present but commented out with a `# TODO: Need to check if it is really required` |

**Confirmed by direct reading of source code**: [FlyPath `wpml/consumer.py`](https://github.com/dronnix-io/FlyPath/blob/main/wpml/consumer.py) and [`wpml/enterprise.py`](https://github.com/dronnix-io/FlyPath/blob/main/wpml/enterprise.py) (contrast namespace), [`hotosm/drone-flightplan`'s `wpml.py`](https://github.com/hotosm/drone-flightplan/blob/main/drone_flightplan/wpml.py).

I could not resolve which namespace DJI Fly on your specific RC2/Mini 5 Pro firmware actually requires or tolerates — **this needs empirical verification on your own hardware** (export a mission built in DJI Fly's own waypoint UI and inspect its `wpmz/template.kml` namespace declaration directly).

### 2.1 Position and height

- **Height modes documented in the enterprise spec:** `EGM96` (absolute, "use the altitude editor"), `relativeToStartPoint`, `aboveGroundLevel` (FlightHub 2 only), `realTimeFollowSurface` (terrain-following, **restricted to M3E/M3T/M3M** in mapping templates only). **Confirmed**, enterprise spec only. [template-kml.md](https://github.com/dji-sdk/Cloud-API-Doc/blob/master/docs/en/60.api-reference/00.dji-wpml/20.template-kml.md)
- **What consumer writers actually use:** both FlyPath and drone-flightplan hardcode `relativeToStartPoint` (`wpml:executeHeightMode` / `wpml:heightMode`) and never emit `EGM96`, `aboveGroundLevel`, or `realTimeFollowSurface`. FlyPath's own README states plainly: "consumer DJI missions fly relative to the takeoff point" — and works around the lack of native terrain-follow by precomputing a **per-waypoint fixed altitude** (`wpml:executeHeight`) from an external DEM, rather than asking the aircraft to terrain-follow live. **Confirmed** (source code + README), both sources agree. [FlyPath README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dronnix-io/FlyPath/main/README.md)
- This matches your existing understanding (per your brief) that DJI Fly has no native terrain-following in waypoint missions — WaypointMap's marketing copy claims "dynamic altitude adjustment... using onboard sensors," but this is doing the same thing as FlyPath (precomputed per-waypoint heights from an external DEM at planning time, or live obstacle-sensor-based adjustment), **not** the enterprise `realTimeFollowSurface` WPML mode. **Inferred** — WaypointMap doesn't publish source, so this is a plausibility judgment, not confirmed against their code.
- **Straight lines vs curves between points:** the consumer format exposes a **per-waypoint** `wpml:waypointTurnMode` + `wpml:useStraightLine` pair (not a single global setting in FlyPath's implementation, though drone-flightplan uses a global `wpml:globalWaypointTurnMode`). FlyPath found empirically that `toPointAndStopWithDiscontinuityCurvature` (straight legs, stop at each point) does **not survive a save/cloud-sync on the DJI RC2** — DJI Fly reconnects the waypoints out of order and scrambles the path (documented as their issue #13) — while `toPointAndPassWithContinuityCurvature` (curved, DJI-native) round-trips correctly. **Confirmed from source code comments describing an observed bug**, a single-project finding — worth confirming independently if your own missions are ever re-saved on the RC2 rather than freshly loaded each time.

### 2.2 Heading and gimbal targeting ("where to look")

- **`waypointHeadingMode` options in the enterprise spec:** `followWayline`, `manually`, `fixed`, `smoothTransition` (target angle via `waypointHeadingAngle`, transitions evenly to the next waypoint's target), `towardPOI` (needs `waypointPoiPoint` lat/lon/alt). **Confirmed**, enterprise spec. [common-element.md](https://github.com/dji-sdk/Cloud-API-Doc/blob/master/docs/en/60.api-reference/00.dji-wpml/40.common-element.md)
- **What consumer writers actually emit:** FlyPath hardcodes `followWayline` with `waypointHeadingAngleEnable = 0` and `waypointPoiPoint = 0,0,0` (i.e., POI targeting is wired into the XML schema but never populated/exercised). drone-flightplan sets `waypointHeadingAngleEnable = 1` and passes through a per-placemark `heading` value, but likewise never uses `towardPOI`. **Neither open-source tool has verified `towardPOI` works on a consumer aircraft** — it's a real element in the schema both tools emit space for, but there is no confirmed evidence it is honoured by DJI Fly on the Mini 5 Pro. **Could not verify** — see below.
- **`gimbalPitchMode`:** enterprise spec has `manual`, `usePointSetting` (pitch transitions evenly toward the next waypoint's setting), `fixed`. FlyPath hardcodes `usePointSetting` at the template level, then sets an **actual per-waypoint pitch value via a field that does not appear anywhere in the official enterprise spec at all**: `wpml:waypointGimbalHeadingParam` containing `wpml:waypointGimbalPitchAngle` and `wpml:waypointGimbalYawAngle`, attached directly to each `Placemark`. This is a consumer-only element (per FlyPath's real-device-verified source) with no enterprise-spec equivalent — the enterprise format instead controls gimbal pitch via a `gimbalRotate` **action**, not a per-waypoint property. **Confirmed structural difference**, from source code; **this is the single clearest piece of evidence that DJI Fly's consumer WPML is a genuinely different (not just trimmed) dialect**, not merely the enterprise spec with unsupported fields ignored.
- Practical implication for your gimbal-pitch requirement (−80°): both approaches (a `gimbalRotate` action at waypoint 0, and/or a per-waypoint `waypointGimbalPitchAngle`) appear in real, device-verified consumer output. Setting both (as FlyPath does at waypoint 0) is the safer bet until you've confirmed on your own hardware which one DJI Fly actually obeys mid-route.

### 2.3 Actions — what DJI Fly actually does at a waypoint

The enterprise spec documents these `actionActuatorFunc` values: `takePhoto`, `startRecord`, `stopRecord`, `focus`, `zoom`, `customDirName`, `gimbalRotate`, `rotateYaw`, `hover`, `gimbalEvenlyRotate`, `accurateShoot` (deprecated on M30/M30T), `orientedShoot`, `panoShot`, `recordPointCloud`. **Every one of these is scoped in the spec's own tables to enterprise aircraft only** — `orientedShoot`, `panoShot`, `accurateShoot`, and `recordPointCloud` are further restricted to specific enterprise models (M30/M30T, M3E/M3T, M3D/M3TD) and are **not shown as supported on any consumer model at all**. **Confirmed**, direct table inspection. [common-element.md](https://github.com/dji-sdk/Cloud-API-Doc/blob/master/docs/en/60.api-reference/00.dji-wpml/40.common-element.md)

**What is actually observed in consumer-targeted, device-verified output:** both independent tools (FlyPath, verified against a real Mini 4 Pro/RC2 mission dump; drone-flightplan, unverified provenance but matching the same pattern) implement **only two action types** for consumer aircraft: `takePhoto` and `gimbalRotate` (drone-flightplan additionally emits `gimbalEvenlyRotate` for smooth in-flight pitch transitions between waypoints). Neither tool implements `startRecord`, `stopRecord`, `hover`, `rotateYaw`, `orientedShoot`, `focus`, or `zoom` for consumer output — but this is evidence of absence in two hobbyist tools, not proof DJI Fly rejects those actions if you tried them. **Could not verify** whether the untried actions work or are silently dropped/rejected on the Mini 5 Pro — flagged below.
Action limits per waypoint: **not documented anywhere**, enterprise or consumer. `actionGroupId`/`actionId` are bounded `[0, 65535]` in the enterprise spec, but that is an ID-space bound, not a "how many actions may fire at one waypoint" limit. **Could not verify.**

- **Action triggers:** enterprise spec defines `reachPoint` (fire on arrival), `betweenAdjacentPoints` (interpolate a gimbal move across a flight segment — spec says "should be used with gimbalEvenlyRotate"), `multipleTiming` (fixed time interval, paired with `takePhoto` for equal-time capture), `multipleDistance` (fixed distance interval, paired with `takePhoto` for equal-distance capture). **Confirmed**, enterprise spec, same source. Consumer tools use only `reachPoint` in their generated output — **could not verify** whether `multipleTiming`/`multipleDistance`/`betweenAdjacentPoints` are honoured by DJI Fly directly from a WPML file (as opposed to being set as a camera-side "interval shooting" setting in the DJI Fly UI itself, which is a separate, non-WPML mechanism — see 2.5).
- **`actionGroupMode`:** enterprise spec documents only one value, `sequence` ("actions within the group execute sequentially"). FlyPath's consumer writer uses **both** `sequence` and `parallel` in real, device-verified output (parallel for a single simultaneous action, sequence for "rotate gimbal, then wait for it, then shoot" at waypoint 0) — meaning `parallel` is a real, working consumer-side value **not documented in the enterprise spec at all**. drone-flightplan also emits `parallel` throughout. **Confirmed** (source), and another concrete example of the two dialects diverging.

### 2.4 How to fly — speed, turns, takeoff, finish, signal loss

- **Speed:** DJI's own support article states waypoint speed range **0.1–15.0 m/s**, default 2.5 m/s, with model-specific ceilings (Mini 4 Pro/Air 3: 12 m/s; Mavic 4 Pro: 15 m/s). **Confirmed.** [Introduction to Waypoint Flight Mode of DJI Fly](https://support.dji.com/help/content?customId=en-us03400007343&spaceId=34&re=US&lang=en&documentType=artical&paperDocType=paper). FlyPath's own device table lists the Mini 5 Pro's ceiling as **15.0 m/s** — consistent with the newer, faster consumer models, but flagged in FlyPath's own data as "community-verified" not "native RC2 mission dump"-verified, i.e. not independently confirmed against real hardware by that project's author. **Confirmed for the general range; the specific 15.0 m/s Mini-5-Pro ceiling is community-sourced, not primary-verified.**
- **Global vs per-waypoint speed:** enterprise spec has both `wpml:autoFlightSpeed` (global) and `wpml:waypointSpeed` (per-waypoint, overrides global for that leg), range `(0, max aircraft speed]`. **Confirmed**, and both consumer tools use per-placemark `waypointSpeed` in their real output, so per-waypoint speed override is real and working on consumer aircraft (at minimum, tested on Mini 4 Pro per FlyPath's provenance note). **Confirmed.**
- **Waypoint turn modes:** enterprise spec lists four: `coordinateTurn`, `toPointAndStopWithDiscontinuityCurvature`, `toPointAndStopWithContinuityCurvature`, `toPointAndPassWithContinuityCurvature`, plus a `waypointTurnDampingDist` for how far ahead of the point the turn begins. **Confirmed**, spec. Consumer tools use only two of the four (see 2.1) — **could not verify** whether `coordinateTurn` or the "stop with continuity curvature" variants work on consumer aircraft at all.
- **`takeOffSecurityHeight`:** enterprise spec bounds it `[1.2, 1500] m` (RC-operated) or `[8, 1500] m` (dock-operated). **Confirmed**, spec; neither consumer tool inspected sets this element at all in their consumer writer — **could not verify** whether DJI Fly on a consumer aircraft exposes/uses it, or relies purely on the RTH-altitude setting configured separately in the DJI Fly app.
- **`finishAction`:** enterprise spec: `goHome`, `noAction`, `autoLand`, `gotoFirstWaypoint`. DJI's own consumer support article: **RTH, Land, Hover, or Back to Start** (RTH default). **Confirmed** from DJI's own article, terminology differs slightly (Hover appears in DJI's consumer UI enumeration; FlyPath's consumer writer only maps three UI labels — Return to Home / Hover in place / Land at last waypoint — to `goHome` / `hover` / `autoLand`, and does not use `gotoFirstWaypoint` or `noAction`). Whether "Hover" as a finish action is truly a distinct WPML value or UI sugar for something else — **could not verify** beyond FlyPath's own mapping.
- **`exitOnRCLost` / signal loss:** enterprise spec: `goContinue` (keep flying the route) or `executeLostAction` (exit and run `executeRCLostAction`: `goBack`, `landing`, `hover`). DJI's consumer support article: **RTH, Land, Hover, or Continue** (RTH default in the DJI Fly UI). **Confirmed**, both sources agree on the four consumer-facing choices; the WPML-level mapping (FlyPath) ties "Return to Home" to `executeLostAction`+`goBack`, "Continue mission" to `goContinue`+`goBack` (the `goBack` here is presumably unused when `goContinue` is selected). **Operationally important:** `hotosm/drone-flightplan`'s writer hardcodes `exitOnRCLost = goContinue` unconditionally — i.e., every mission it generates tells the aircraft to keep flying the planned route on signal loss, regardless of any setting, rather than defaulting to DJI Fly's own RTH default. **Confirmed by direct source reading** — this is a fact about your own tool's current output, worth a deliberate decision rather than an accident.
- **`flyToWaylineMode`:** enterprise spec: `safely` (climb then level to first waypoint) or `pointToPoint` (tilt directly toward it). **Confirmed**, spec; both consumer tools hardcode `safely`. **Confirmed** consumer default.

### 2.5 Camera settings — mission file vs. DJI Fly app

- **Resolution (12 MP vs 50 MP), photo format (JPEG vs DNG), exposure mode, ISO, shutter, EV, white balance:** **none of these appear anywhere in the WPML schema**, enterprise or consumer — confirmed by exhaustively reading every documented `actionActuatorFuncParam` block in the enterprise spec (none of `takePhoto`, `startRecord`, `focus`, `zoom` carry exposure/ISO/WB/format parameters) and by the FlyPath README's own explicit statement: **"The gimbal is fixed at nadir (-90 degrees) for both mission types, so there is no Camera Settings section"** in their planning UI — i.e., the tool that most closely mirrors what DJI Fly natively supports treats camera settings as entirely out-of-band. **Confirmed**: these settings must be configured in the DJI Fly app's camera panel before starting the mission; they cannot be scripted per-waypoint or globally inside a WPML KMZ.
- **50 MP fastest capture interval:** conflicting figures found. A camera-spec aggregator states the 50 MP mode's timed-interval range is "2 to 60 seconds depending on resolution" (implying 2 s may only apply at lower resolution, not 50 MP specifically) — **secondary source, imprecise**. [DJI Mini 5 Pro Specs guide](https://www.diyphotography.net/dji-mini-5-pro-specs-guide/). Independently, FlyPath's hardware table lists `min_shoot_interval_s: 5.0` for the Mini 5 Pro at 50 MP (8192×6144), versus `2.0` for the 12 MP Mini 3/4 Pro — and flags this row as "community-verified," not tested against real RC2 hardware by that project. **Could not verify the exact fastest 50 MP interval with confidence; treat 5 s as the safer planning assumption, confirm empirically against your own aircraft** (a hyperlapse/interval-shooting test on the ground will settle this in minutes).
- **JPEG vs DNG:** both are supported photo formats on the Mini 5 Pro per general spec write-ups, but which one is active for a waypoint mission's `takePhoto` action is entirely determined by the DJI Fly camera panel setting at the time of the flight, not by the mission file. **Inferred from the same "no camera settings in WPML" finding above** — not separately confirmed for format specifically, but follows directly from the same evidence.

### 2.6 What DJI Fly's own generated KMZ proves

I could not obtain a genuine DJI-Fly-exported sample KMZ directly (no such file was retrievable via web search or fetch in this session — DJI does not publish one, and no forum post I found included a full raw XML dump). The best available substitute is FlyPath's explicit claim, in its own source code header comment, of having been **"verified against a DJI Mini 4 Pro + DJI RC2 native mission dump"** — i.e., its author did obtain and inspect a real DJI-Fly-generated KMZ and matched their writer's output to it. I'm treating that comment as credible primary-adjacent evidence (a named, working open-source tool built and shipped against real hardware output) but it is **secondhand** — I have not personally inspected the raw KMZ. **This is the single highest-value thing to verify yourself**: build a short waypoint mission directly in DJI Fly's own UI on your RC2, export/locate its KMZ (DJI Fly stores each mission as a KMZ file locally; USB-copy it off), and diff it against what `drone-flightplan` produces. That will settle the open namespace question (2.0), the `template.kml`-required-or-not question (2.0/2.1), and which actions/fields are actually accepted, definitively, for your exact aircraft and firmware — no amount of further web research will substitute for that one file.

### 2.7 Waypoint/mission-level limits

- **Maximum waypoints per mission:** **200**, stated by both DJI's own support article and independently by WaypointMap and FlyPath's documentation (which auto-splits larger missions across multiple battery-flights at this cap). **Confirmed, multiple independent sources.** [DJI support article](https://support.dji.com/help/content?customId=en-us03400007343&spaceId=34&re=US&lang=en&documentType=artical&paperDocType=paper), [WaypointMap](https://www.waypointmap.com/drone/dji-mini-5-pro-automated-mission-flight-planning), [FlyPath README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dronnix-io/FlyPath/main/README.md)
- **Gimbal pitch range:** DJI's spec sheets/reviews report a mechanical range of roughly **−135° to +80°**, with a **controllable (user-commandable) range of about −90° to +55°** on the Mini 5 Pro. **Confirmed from secondary spec-aggregation sources, not DJI's own spec page directly** (I could not load a definitive first-party numeric gimbal-pitch spec table on dji.com in this session — its specs are split across marketing pages that emphasize the 225° roll feature more than pitch numbers). Your −80° operating point is safely within every reported range.
- **Mission resume after battery swap:** general DJI-ecosystem behavior (documented for other DJI waypoint-capable aircraft, and consistent with community reporting for DJI Fly): after a battery swap and RC reconnect, DJI Fly offers to **resume the paused mission** — options typically include continue from the interruption point, restart from the beginning, or restart from a chosen waypoint index — but the resume state is lost if a different mission is started or the RC is powered off in between. **Inferred from community/support documentation on the general DJI Fly waypoint resume mechanism, not confirmed specifically for the Mini 5 Pro in DJI's own documentation** — worth a one-time field test before relying on it operationally.
- **Obstacle avoidance during missions:** the Mini 5 Pro has omnidirectional obstacle sensing including a forward LiDAR (rated to work down to 1 lux, i.e., functions at night). **Confirmed as a hardware capability** from DJI's own product announcement/marketing material. Whether obstacle avoidance actively deviates the aircraft from a planned waypoint path (vs. only braking/stopping) during an automated mission specifically — **could not verify** from any source in this session; this is a meaningful open question for flying near structures/equipment on a construction site and should be tested deliberately (e.g., a controlled low-risk pass near a known obstacle) rather than assumed either way.

### Building / obstacle heights

- NRCan has released a **lidar-derived building-footprint layer with height/elevation attributes**, initial release ~1 million footprints, covering **Manitoba, Nova Scotia, Alberta, and British Columbia** — i.e., partial, not national. **Confirmed.** [Talk-ca mailing list: NRC building footprints from lidar](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/talk-ca/2019-April/009291.html)
- OpenStreetMap `building:height` / `building:levels` tags exist for some Canadian buildings but coverage is sparse and crowd-sourced/inconsistent; Microsoft's global building-footprints dataset (ODbL) includes Canada but is footprints, not reliably heights. **Confirmed** existence of these sources; **not confirmed** as sufficient/complete coverage for any specific construction site — treat as "check what's there, don't assume it's there."
- **No single authoritative, complete, national source of building heights for Canada was found.** For your use case (flying around specific known construction sites), the most reliable obstacle-height source is likely to be **your own first flight's photogrammetry/lidar** at each site, used to build a local obstacle model for subsequent visits — not a national dataset. **Inferred**, not a citation.

### Airspace data

- **NAV CANADA's own data cannot be redistributed.** The NRC's Drone Site Selection Tool explicitly states: "The licensing terms for the Nav Canada data used to derive the map shapes prohibit re-distribution of the underlying data at this time." **Confirmed**, matches what you'd already found. [NRC Drone Site Selection Tool](https://cnrc.canada.ca/en/drone-tool/)
- **No API for the NRC tool** — a support Q&A on the tool states plainly that third-party developer database access is not available ("Sorry, but no"). **Confirmed**, matches your prior finding.
- **Transport Canada aerodrome/heliport data** *is* open and downloadable: "Canadian Airports with Air Navigation Services" is published as **Shapefile (English/French) and ESRI REST/WMS services** on the Open Government Portal. **Confirmed.** [Open Government Portal dataset](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/3a1eb6ef-6054-4f9d-b1f6-c30322cd7abf)
- **OpenAIP** (crowd-sourced worldwide aeronautical database) publishes Canada airspace data with a documented API (`docs.openaip.net`), free for private use, exportable as GeoJSON/JSON/MBTiles/OpenAir among other formats. **Confirmed the API and export formats exist**; **not confirmed** how complete or current its Canadian restricted/advisory-airspace coverage is versus NAV CANADA's own designated-airspace handbook — OpenAIP is crowd-maintained, so treat it as a useful machine-readable *starting point*, not an authoritative replacement for checking NOTAMs and the NRC tool by hand before each flight. [OpenAIP](https://www.openaip.net/), [OpenAIP API docs](https://docs.openaip.net/)
- **National parks boundaries:** Parks Canada publishes "National Parks and National Park Reserves of Canada — Legislative Boundaries" and "Places administered by Parks Canada" on the Open Government Portal, in Shapefile/GeoJSON/KML/CSV and as a web service. **Confirmed.** [Open Government Portal — Parks Canada legislative boundaries](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/9e1507cd-f25c-4c64-995b-6563bf9d65bd), [Places administered by Parks Canada](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/e1f0c975-f40c-4313-9be2-beb951e35f4e)

**Bottom line for Q3 (inferred synthesis):** you can build a fully programmatic pre-flight terrain+obstacle+parks layer (HRDEM/MRDEM + Parks Canada boundaries + Transport Canada aerodromes, all machine-readable with open licences), but airspace restriction data specifically has no authoritative machine-readable Canadian source — OpenAIP is the closest thing, with the caveat that it's crowd-sourced and not licensed for redistribution parity with NAV CANADA's own tool. A manual NRC tool / NOTAM check before each flight remains necessary regardless of what you automate.

## Q4 — Verification

### Pre-flight: footprint / overlap / GSD from a plan + DEM

- **QGIS "Flight Planner" plugin**: computes projection-centre, waypoint, photo-range, and flight-line layers from a photogrammetric flight plan; outputs include real photo coverage (footprint), a raster of overlapping-image counts, and a GSD raster. **Confirmed generically capable of exactly what you need**, but it is built around its own mission-planning workflow, not around ingesting an arbitrary DJI WPML KMZ — you would likely need to adapt it (or extract waypoints/altitude/gimbal-pitch from your own KMZ and feed them in) rather than use it as a drop-in checker. [QGIS Flight Planner plugin page](https://plugins.qgis.org/plugins/flight_planner/)
- **QGroundControl** (PX4/ArduPilot-oriented, not DJI): its Survey/Structure Scan planning views compute and display per-image camera footprints and overlap live during mission planning, from camera sensor/focal-length specs plus altitude, assuming flat ground unless corrected — the same computation you'd want, but again not built to import a DJI WPML file. **Confirmed** capability exists; **not directly usable on your KMZ files without adaptation.** [QGC Survey docs](https://docs.qgroundcontrol.com/master/en/qgc-user-guide/plan_view/pattern_survey.html)
- **No confirmed, ready-made open-source tool that ingests a DJI WPML KMZ + a DEM and outputs footprint/overlap/GSD directly** was found. Given you already have `drone-flightplan`'s own waypoint/altitude generation code and camera geometry, the lowest-effort path (my inference, not a citation) is likely to write this check as a small script against your own pipeline's intermediate GeoJSON/waypoint data (which you already have before it becomes WPML), rather than trying to reverse-parse your own KMZ output.

### Post-flight: comparing a flight to its plan via DJI EXIF/XMP

- DJI writes telemetry into each photo's XMP under the `http://www.dji.com/drone-dji/1.0/` namespace, readable via ExifTool: `GPSLatitude`/`GPSLongitude` (standard EXIF GPS block), `AbsoluteAltitude` (MSL), `RelativeAltitude` (above takeoff point), `GimbalPitchDegree`/`GimbalRollDegree`/`GimbalYawDegree`, `FlightPitchDegree`/`FlightRollDegree`/`FlightYawDegree`. **Confirmed directly from ExifTool's own source**: [`DJI.pm` on GitHub](https://github.com/exiftool/exiftool/blob/master/lib/Image/ExifTool/DJI.pm) — and I confirmed the Mini 5 Pro specifically has dedicated tag mappings in the same file (`dvtm_Mini5Pro_*` entries for video telemetry, plus the shared static XMP tags above for stills), so ExifTool support for your exact aircraft is not in question.
- Comparison approach (**inferred workflow**, not itself a cited method): for each captured photo, match it to its nearest planned waypoint/action by sequence or by GPS proximity, then diff actual vs. planned: horizontal position (GPS vs. waypoint lon/lat), `RelativeAltitude` vs. planned `executeHeight`, `GimbalPitchDegree` vs. planned gimbal target, and photo timestamps vs. expected interval/spacing (to catch missed or duplicated shots, or a flight that ran slower/faster than planned). This is standard photogrammetry QA practice but I did not find a single named tool or spec that performs exactly this DJI-KMZ-vs-EXIF diff — you'd be writing it yourself against ExifTool's output and your own waypoint list.

### Post-flight: ODM's own report figures

- WebODM/ODM's Quality Report includes: an **overlap heatmap/diagram** (goal: maximize "5+ overlap" green coverage), and **error tables** broken into GPS (one measurement per geotagged image), GCP (one per GCP), and 3D (1000 sampled measurements from the sparse point cloud, via reprojection + iterative worst-case triangulation), each split into X/Y (horizontal) and Z (vertical) components plus a Total. The docs explicitly caveat that these are estimates and **not a substitute for independent checkpoints**, and that GPS-measurement uncertainty isn't modeled into the error estimate. **Confirmed.** [WebODM Quality Reports reference](https://docs.webodm.net/references/quality-reports/)
- GSD is also reported as a run parameter/output in ODM's standard report, consistent with the "Flying Tips"/"map-accuracy" docs cited under Q1.

### Acceptance criteria — "this flight met the plan"

- **ASPRS Edition 2 (2023/2024)** is the closest thing to a formal, named standard for this, now RMSE-only (dropped the older 95%-confidence convention) and with a dedicated UAS addendum — but I could not access the actual threshold numbers (paywalled). **Confirmed the standard exists and is RMSE-based; could not verify its specific pass/fail thresholds.**
- Commonly-cited (but **secondary/blog-sourced, not a standards body**) rules of thumb I found: mean tie-point reprojection error **< ~1.0 px** considered acceptable for survey-grade work, **> ~1.5 px** indicating a systemic problem (bad tie points, wrong camera calibration, or mixed datasets); computed forward/side overlap at every flight line should meet or exceed the planned floor after terrain correction; feature-poor surfaces (bare earth, snow, water edges) reportedly show real degradation below 70–80% overlap. **Flagged as inferred/secondary** — these numbers came from an industry blog (aerocartwright.com), not ASPRS, Pix4D, or ODM directly, and should be treated as a reasonable starting heuristic rather than a defensible standard if accuracy claims ever need to be defended to a client or regulator.
- **Practical recommendation (inferred):** build your own acceptance gate from the sources that *are* primary and confirmed — ODM's own quality-report thresholds (aim to maximize green/5+ overlap coverage, watch the reported reprojection-error and GPS/GCP error tables) plus a straightforward EXIF-vs-plan diff (position, altitude, gimbal pitch, and timing) — and treat ASPRS Edition 2 as the standard to formally adopt once you can access its actual UAS-addendum numbers, rather than trying to invent your own numeric threshold from blog sources.
